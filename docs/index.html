The use case

Banks decide in milliseconds, and still get it wrong most of the time.

Why legacy monitoring struggles

  • Static rules such as "flag any cash deposit over $10,000" are easy to game.
  • Industry estimates put false-positive rates at 95–99%.
  • Analysts spend most of their time copying data between systems instead of investigating.
  • Launderers adapt: $9,500 deposits, five hops in 45 seconds, shell companies holding 24.5% each.

Why not one big model?

  • Cost and latency differ by orders of magnitude. A string match takes microseconds, a graph model milliseconds, an LLM investigation seconds to minutes.
  • Some decisions must be deterministic. A sanctions hit is a legal obligation, not a probability.
  • Accountability can't be delegated. A suspicious activity report (SAR) is a legal filing, so a qualified human must stand behind it.
The architecture

A cascade, not a single model.

Most transactions are approved within milliseconds. A small fraction becomes an alert. Only those alerts reach the expensive agent swarm, and only a human can file.

Data sovereignty

A hybrid-cloud boundary built around privacy.

Steady, high-volume work runs on-premises on OpenStack. Burst-heavy reasoning and retraining run on Google Cloud. Raw names, national IDs and account numbers never cross the line.

On-premises · OpenStack

  • Kafka ingestion, ISO 20022 canonical schema
  • Tier 1 rules engine and sanctions screening
  • PII tokenization gateway and token vault

Tier 2 · Triage gate

Three models disagree on purpose.

A supervised model asks "have I seen this before?", an autoencoder asks "is this unlike anything normal?", and a graph scorer asks "does the surrounding network look wrong?" Their blend decides where the transaction goes next.

Tier 3

A swarm of specialists, planned as a DAG.

For alerts with S_final ≥ 0.35, a Supervisor writes an execution plan of five dependent tasks. Independent tasks run first; the Compliance Critic audits the final narrative.

1

Entity Resolution & KYC

Clusters shared devices and unrolls ownership trees: C_effective = Π M_ownership. Four shells at ~24% each still resolve to one person controlling 100%.

2

Transaction Forensics

Dwell time under 120 s, pass-through ratio above 0.90, peeling chains, circular loops and sub-$10k structuring.

3

OSINT & Adverse Media

Searches media for parties and unmasked controllers; flags trade-based laundering when invoice price deviates > 25% from benchmark.

4

SAR Synthesis

Drafts a five-part narrative, maps typologies to suspicious-activity codes and builds the event timeline.

5

Compliance Critic

Checks every claim against the evidence dossier. The design goal is entailment ≥ 0.90 per claim, to eliminate hallucinated allegations.

★

Supervisor

Plans the DAG, runs a circuit breaker (three failures → straight to a human) and may auto-clear only with no severe evidence and S_final < 0.45.

Step by step

Two transactions, two very different paths.

    Coverage

    Eight evasion typologies, each with a one-click simulation.

    Governance

    Built to be explained and audited.

    Cryptographic audit ledger

    Every stage appends a SHA-256 hash-chained block. Change any historical record and every later hash breaks; a verify endpoint finds the first corrupted block.

    Model risk management

    A registry tracks champion / challenger / shadow models in the spirit of SR 11-7, and a shadow validator flags transactions where models differ by more than 0.15.

    Drift detection

    Population Stability Index and Wasserstein distance for covariate drift, Page-Hinkley for concept drift, with a recommended action.

    Human-only filing

    Only a case in SAR_APPROVED can be submitted. Overrides require a reason code and written justification. Enforced in code, not convention.

    Five-dimension metrics

    Business impact, system engineering, RAG triad, agentic behavior and ML quality (PR-AUC, calibration, Brier score) are all measured.

    Prompt governance

    Versioned prompt templates with a groundedness benchmark and one-call rollback to a certified earlier version.

    Transparency

    What is real, and what is simulated.

    This is a reference architecture and prototype, not a production AML system. Here is the honest picture.

    Implemented & tested

    • Full tier cascade and triage logic, end to end
    • Sanctions matcher (Jaro-Winkler, Soundex, aliases) and CTR / wire / velocity / jurisdiction rules
    • Feature store, knowledge graph, tokenizer and tamper-evident audit chain
    • Recursive UBO unroller, TBML price check, SAR payload builder
    • Human approve / override workflow with an enforced state machine
    • PSI, Wasserstein, Page-Hinkley, ECE and Brier calculations
    • Circuit breaker and safe-degradation fallback
    • 50 automated tests

    Simulated stand-ins

    • Kafka, Redis, Neo4j, BigQuery and Vertex AI are in-memory structures and JSON files
    • Models are real scikit-learn but fitted at startup on synthetic data
    • The "GNN" is a rule-weighted topological scorer, not a trained RGCN / GAT
    • Agents are deterministic Python components; no LLM is called yet
    • The Critic uses lexical overlap, not true entailment
    • Dashboard business / throughput numbers are seeded samples and design targets, not benchmarks
    • Regulator acceptance in the SAR gateway is simulated
    Next steps on the roadmap: LLM-driven tool-using agents on a zero-retention endpoint, an NLI-based critic, a trained graph model, real labeled data with temporal backtesting, and production infrastructure.
